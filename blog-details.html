Phase One Workflow: 150MP Editorial Standards

Marcus Ellison
Founder & Creative Director
April 10, 2025
8 min read
4,821 views

In the world of high-end editorial and commercial advertising, resolution is only half the battle. The true challenge lies in maintaining color integrity and data throughput when shooting at 150MP. Whether you're utilizing the Phase One IQ4 or the latest GFX 100II, your digital workflow is the bottleneck between a good image and a global campaign standard.

After hosting 5,200+ high-resolution sessions at HEPS, we've identified the three pillars of a stable professional workflow: Hardware Synchronization, Color Management, and Tethered Optimization.

"Your price is a signal. When you charge $50 for a session, you're telling the market you're worth $50 - regardless of your actual skill." - Marcus Ellison, HEPS Founder

Pillar 1: Tethered Optimization & Data Throughput

When each RAW file exceeds 200MB, traditional USB protocols often fail. To maintain 150MP standards, HEPS utilizes dedicated 10Gbps fiber bridges between the studio floor and the DIT station.

  • Thunderbolt 4 Bridging: Eliminating the lag between shutter release and screen preview
  • Capture One 23 Live: Real-time client review across localized fiber networks
  • SSD RAID Arrays: Redundant, high-speed writing to prevent data corruption
  • UPS Power Backup: Ensuring the tether never breaks during critical power fluctuations

Optimizing your tethered connection isn't just about speed; it's about production reliability. A single dropped connection during a celebrity session can cost thousands in lost crew time.

Step 2: Build a Three-Tier Price Architecture

Offering a single price package is one of the biggest pricing mistakes photographers make. Clients have different needs and budgets - a single price either leaves money on the table from premium clients or scares away budget clients unnecessarily.

The three-tier model creates a natural anchoring effect. The middle tier feels like the "safe" choice, the lower tier makes the middle look great value, and the upper tier signals your premium positioning.

Tier 1: Entry (Essential)

Designed to be accessible without being cheap. This tier typically covers 2-4 hours of shooting, one edited look, and digital delivery only. Price it at 120-150% of your CODB per day rate, divided proportionally for the hours offered.

Tier 2: Core (Signature)

This is your money-maker and typically accounts for 60-70% of bookings. It should include your full suite of services, a healthy number of edits, and at least one round of revisions. Price it at 200-250% of your CODB floor.

Tier 3: Premium (Elite)

This tier exists to make your Signature tier look like great value. It should include everything, unlimited edits, rush delivery, physical prints, and access to your personal network. Price it at 350%+ of your CODB floor. Some photographers make 20% of their revenue from Elite bookings - money they previously never captured.

Step 3: Price for the Client's Outcome, Not Your Input

Amateur photographers price based on time and effort. Professional photographers price based on the value of the outcome to the client. A 2-hour product shoot for a funded startup isn't worth $300 - it's worth the revenue those images will generate across their marketing campaigns.

Start asking discovery questions during your inquiry phase: "What is this content being used for?" and "What's the campaign budget?" This context lets you position your fee appropriately - and confidently defend it.

Step 4: Raise Your Rates Annually

Inflation alone demands a minimum 3-5% annual price increase. But your experience, reputation, and portfolio also compound in value each year. Build a "rate review" into your calendar every January. Most photographers find that a 10-15% annual increase rarely costs them a single existing client.

"Price increases are not about greed. They're about respecting the compounding value of your craft." - Sofia Laurent, HEPS Studio Manager

Conclusion

Pricing is not a one-time decision - it's an ongoing business discipline. Start with your CODB, build a tiered architecture, shift from input-based to outcome-based thinking, and commit to annual reviews. The photographers thriving in 2025 are those who treat pricing as seriously as they treat their craft.
